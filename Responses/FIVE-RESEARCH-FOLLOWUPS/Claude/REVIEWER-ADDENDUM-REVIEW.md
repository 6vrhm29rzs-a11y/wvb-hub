Remaining issues to resolve internally, without another external round:
1. IMPORTANT: the claim that any later named ace lets us reconstruct every serving team backwards to the first rally is not generally true. With complete ordinary rally winners, server-team at rally t>1 equals winner at t-1. Either team could have served rally 1 and then lost/won it; a later anchor does not invert that lost information. Preserve first-serve unknown unless directly observed or independently established via cross-set rules and a known first serve. Administrative points and corrections need explicit handling. This is a logic counterexample, not a request to implement a deriver.
2. Calling a 2025 rerun clean remains too broad after 2025 informed model/feature selection. It can diagnose development behavior; a future frozen evaluation supplies stronger confirmation. A last-pre-match selection also differs from the existing first-write log.
3. Appendix A again calls derived k=13.52 the live k. Live is 10. Distinguish theoretical estimate, historical experiment value, and implemented setting.
4. The text alternates between acknowledging the venue classifier and saying neutral detection is not built. Forecast classifier exists; rating adjustment remains nominal. Hand-verified 2025 venue labels are additional work, not data already on hand.
5. Shared frozen opponent estimates can still correlate split halves even when the focal team's games are excluded. Spearman–Brown needs its assumptions; chronological skill drift, unequal exposure, dyadic/team dependence and split construction matter. Team-pair clusters are not automatically the same as dependence from a team appearing in many different pairings. DEFF/SESOI need an explicit estimator and units, not a generic formula.
6. Pick flips/top-25 movement are not automatically probability-forecast improvement. A model can improve calibrated confidence without flipping a winner. Do not require Cody to invent a statistical threshold.
7. Weighting by realized exposure changes the estimand; it does not necessarily create directional bias against dominant teams under every correctly specified likelihood. Treat that as a design concern, not a theorem.
8. Some legal/access statements depend on old snippets, mirrors or subagent reports with date conflicts. Preserve current project source boundaries; this report is not a fresh definitive terms determination. Do not assert universal legal prohibition from incomplete access.
9. The 58–66% illustrative interval is approximately the naive binomial interval; do not describe it as the demonstrated match-clustered interval. No independent current NCAA benchmark was established.

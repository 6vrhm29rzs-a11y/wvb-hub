# POWER: Critical Research Review
## Volleyball analytics, data access, AI tools, and reliable automation

**Prepared for Cody, Reviewer, and Builder · September 26, 2026**  
**Status: research for joint review—not an implementation plan or approval.**

This document evaluates the reviewed research packet and current primary-source documentation. It does not reproduce the packet's experiments, certify the working repository, or test any vendor account. No project code, ratings, services, accounts, schedules, or purchases were changed.

**Evidence key.** **P** = packet or dated user-provided system evidence; **V** = verified in an external primary source; **H** = hypothesis or analytical inference; **R** = recommendation; **U** = unresolved. A vendor's documented capability is not an independently measured accuracy result. Source identifiers refer to the source register in Section 8 and link to the primary-source pages.

**Priority key.** **TRY NOW** means the best candidate for a small trial *after joint approval*. **RESEARCH NEXT** means resolve access or evidence first. **LATER** means useful only after a dependency is met. **NOT WORTH IT** means poor fit for the present project—not a universal judgment about the product.

## 1. Conclusions

### 1.1 The main recommendation

**R — Preserve live POWER while investigating two complementary paths:** a better-evaluated predictive system using the data already held, and a richer explanatory system that makes passing, setting, attacking, serving, and availability observable. Neither path requires committing to a database migration, another premium AI subscription, or a national licensed feed now.

The packet supports neither “box scores have no further predictive value” nor “ten weighted metrics will improve forecasts.” Its experiments support a narrower conclusion: particular tested formulations produced small, uncertain, or negative gains under particular evaluation designs. All ten metrics should remain candidates for explanation and properly specified predictive research. [P0: Reviewer addendum; Current Model §7]

### 1.3 What I would prioritize—and what I would not

**TRY NOW, subject to approval:** an exact-baseline/as-of audit; one descriptive team dossier using existing counts; an existing-2025-PBP serving study; and a sealed extraction benchmark comparing deterministic code, the already-available local model, and at most one metered cloud model.

**RESEARCH NEXT:** a permissioned sample of contact-level data, preferably for the Stanford setter case; a vendor coverage-and-rights questionnaire; a set-aware strength model; and reliability estimates by opportunity count rather than universal match thresholds.

**LATER:** national contact-value rankings, broad player-availability adjustments, paid orchestration, persistent autonomous research agents, and a shared production database.

**NOT WORTH IT now:** buying every AI subscription, rebuilding the hub around another project's dashboard, using social search as an authoritative scoreboard, or trying to automate a source this project is prohibited from accessing.

## 2. Volleyball-model evidence and improvements

### 2.1 What the packet establishes—and what it does not

**P — Exact live reference.** The documented rating blends a preseason projection with season evidence using `w = n / (n + 10)`. With both teams' attack counts, match evidence is 75% opponent-adjusted scoreboard margin per set and 25% opponent-adjusted hitting-efficiency difference; otherwise it is margin-only. Opponent ratings are updated four times. The rating's home adjustment uses nominal home/away labels even at neutral events. Forecast conversion is a separate calibrated stage. [P0: Current Model §§2–4]

The documented hitting-scale helper returns 0.100 because of a floor, whereas its original calibration population yielded an unfloored value near 0.0668. However, the earlier hitting-weight receipt used a different prior blend, `k = 13.5`. The fully current configuration was not established by that receipt alone. The proposed restricted D-I population also requires a fresh scale estimate. [P0: Reviewer addendum items 2–3]

**R — Replace the proposed two-arm C1 comparison with an attribution-preserving diagnostic.** At minimum:

| Arm | Population and scale | Question answered |
|---|---|---|
| A | Exact live calibration population, current floor, live `k = 10`, current forecast mapping | What are we actually comparing against? |
| B | Countable D-I calibration population, same floor rule; all recomputed dependencies disclosed | What changes from population restriction alone? |
| C | Same population as B, freshly estimated unfloored scale | What changes from removing the floor on that population? |

An optional fourth arm, original population plus unfloored scale, completes the small factorial comparison; this is not a broad parameter search. Initially hold the forecast mapping fixed to expose rating changes. A second, explicitly separate exercise may recalibrate each arm using training-only data. If the floor remains preferable, describe it as regularization, not a measured historical spread. **Cost/access:** existing frozen files and local compute; no service fee. **Fallback:** keep A. **Success:** each change has an interpretable paired comparison, not merely a prettier ranking.

**P — Weak results are not a universal veto.** Round 2's richer candidate had calibrated log loss 0.4640 versus 0.4667 for its development baseline, with a confidence interval spanning no improvement. The W/L-only candidate performed worse but reached tuning-grid edges. Earlier hitting gains and later indistinguishability both remain on record. [P0: Current Model §7]

**R — Treat 2025 as development evidence.** Reused full-season choices and repeated checkpoint forecasts can make confidence intervals look more decisive than genuine forward generalization warrants. Keep prior-only and a reasonable W/L model as references; do not call the latter family disproved. Report a frozen live replica alongside any cleaned or refitted baseline.

### 2.2 What external research contributes

**V — Contact-level player value, NCAA women.** Scott Powers, Luke Stancil, and Naomi Consiglio's February 2, 2024 preprint uses 2022 D-I women's charted data: 4,147 matches, over 600,000 points, and over five million contacts. It models rally states and allocates changes in point-win probability, with hierarchical adjustment for opposing players in adversarial contacts. Its grading representation is not the forum's documented 0–3 scale. **Boundary:** this is evidence for contextual skill valuation, not demonstrated superiority to POWER on future match forecasts; access rights to its underlying charted corpus are not established here. [S01]

**V — Volleyball-specific score likelihood.** Leonardo Egidi and Ioannis Ntzoufras's November 5, 2019 preprint models set wins and conditional losing-team points, with special treatment of extra points. Its Italian men's SuperLega application includes a first-half/second-half predictive exercise. **Boundary:** different sex, league, schedule, and sample; neither its fitted home effect nor its forecasting performance transfers automatically to NCAA women. Its useful contribution is a likelihood respecting volleyball's score structure. [S02]

**V — Hierarchical prediction in women's professional volleyball.** Andrea Gabrio's November 20, 2019 preprint evaluates alternative Bayesian hierarchical specifications using Italian women's Serie A1 2017–18. **Boundary:** it does not validate NCAA parameters or an incremental gain over this hub's margin-based baseline. It supports hierarchical modeling as a credible research family, not a selected replacement. [S03]

**V — Open analytics building blocks.** OpenVolley's `datavolley` reads `.dvw` scouting files and `.vsm` VolleyStation files. `ovlytics` includes data augmentation and setter-choice analysis. Their authors publish MIT-licensed software. **Boundary:** software availability supplies neither scouting observations nor permission to redistribute another owner's data. [S08], [S09]

**U — Public rating-system folklore.** This review did not establish primary documentation for Pablo's exact model specification, prior carryover, blowout treatment, or a transferable NCAA women's home-advantage estimate. RichKern's public page establishes a subscription service, not those parameters. Nor did this review establish a reproducible, target-matched future-forecast comparison for Massey, VolleyDork, or the other external reference systems named in the packet. Use them as dated external benchmarks only when access and archive rights permit; do not borrow undocumented constants. [S10]; [P0: §F]

### 2.3 Fresh hypothesis: make the model understand opportunities and phases

**H — A component can be useful because it changes the interpretation of a result, not because it deserves an additive bonus.** A high-error, high-kill outside hitter and a low-error middle can have identical hitting efficiency while playing very different roles. A serving run can reflect the block behind the server. A dig total can reflect opponent attack volume or failure to terminate earlier rallies. The packet recognizes these distinctions; the modeling opportunity is to represent them explicitly. [P0: ten-metric discussion]

**R — Start with a small, partially pooled count model.** For attacks, model **kill / error / continued rally** as mutually exclusive outcomes, with opponent context and conservative team/player effects. Keep attack share, role labels, and sample size visible. Add actual attack location or in-system status only where observed. For a predictive trial, use lagged, past-only component estimates to explain residual forecast error beyond the live rating—not same-match box scores to “predict” that match.

This separates the question “which processes differ?” from “does that difference predict the next match?” It also avoids treating hitting%, kill%, kills/set, and earned PPS as independent evidence when they share algebraic components. **Access/cost:** existing box counts; $0 additional software required. **Small trial:** six team dossiers and one prespecified lagged component family. **Success:** defensible denominators, useful explanations, and an honest forward comparison. **Fallback:** descriptive-only display.

| Metric family | Useful denominator or decomposition now | What it cannot establish |
|---|---|---|
| Kills and hitting | K/TA, E/TA, TA/set, player share of team TA; sum raw counts first | Actual set quality, attack location, or causal hitter value |
| Serving | Aces/serve attempts and errors/serve attempts; later team rally wins on the player's serves | Isolated server quality or a universal ace:error target |
| Reception | Reception errors/recorded reception attempts; explicit coverage | A passing grade, perfect-pass share, or setter options |
| Digging | Digs/opponent TA as an explicitly labeled exposure proxy; opponent volume beside it | True “dig success”: not every opponent attempt is diggable |
| Blocking | Team scoring blocks/opponent TA; distinguish team scores from individual participation | Touches, funneling, coverage, or who caused a stuff block |
| Setting | Assists alongside team kills, setter participation, and attack distribution | Set quality or a causal ranking of setters |
| Earned PPS and points allowed | K + aces + team blocks per set, separate from scoreboard margin | An additive accounting ledger containing overlapping errors/blocks |

The denominators above are proposed analytical conventions unless already defined in the packet, not a claim of one universally standardized volleyball metric set. A source dictionary must accompany each. In particular, **our attack being blocked may count as their block and our attack error**; summing both as separate point losses double-counts the event. [P0: Reviewer addendum item 8; metrics §§1–10]

### 2.4 Fresh hypothesis: serving and first-ball side-out deserve their own study

**R — Use the existing 2025 PBP before pursuing 2026 acquisition.** Estimate the team's rally-win percentage while each player serves, including service errors in the denominator, with opponent and serving-rotation context. Report attempts and intervals. The serving six reconstructed from the packet is not necessarily the on-court six, and a listed starter order is not rotation order. [P0: C6; inventory `rotations_2025`]

**H — First-ball side-out and transition conversion could reveal different strengths hidden by total margin.** They require a rally sequence capable of distinguishing an immediate first attack from a later transition; do not relabel reception errors as first-ball side-out. A first useful comparison is whether server performance persists across matched blocks of opponents and rotations, then whether that estimate predicts *future* serving outcomes.

**Reliability is an empirical question.** There is no supported “stabilizes after X matches” answer here. Estimate it by opportunity count and context. For scale, an ordinary independent-binomial approximation at 40% on 50 serves has standard error about 6.9 percentage points—roughly ±14 points for a crude 95% interval. Actual service runs and shared lineups violate independence, so this is not a valid final player interval. Use clustered or hierarchical estimates and show the effective sample limitation. This calculation illustrates why rankings from a few runs are fragile.

### 2.5 Fresh hypothesis: set-aware evidence and adaptive uncertainty

**H — Averaging match point margin across sets discards informative structure.** A short deciding set and a regulation-length set have different scoring opportunities; match duration is itself related to team parity. The current forecast simulator may respect match rules while the *rating input* still collapses those differences. A set-level likelihood with legal score boundaries and deuce handling is a substantively different candidate from adding more box-score weights. [P0: Current Model §§3–4]; [S02]

**R — Compare one set-aware candidate with the exact live baseline.** Preserve opponent adjustment, separate neutral-site status, and shrink noisy team changes. Evaluate scoreline probabilities as well as match probabilities. Do not resurrect arbitrary margin caps merely because another system reportedly uses them: the packet already contains negative or inconclusive cap tests.

**H — Equal match counts need not imply equal knowledge.** Repeated matches within a weakly connected conference schedule may tell us less about national ordering than diverse cross-conference opponents. Roster continuity, a verified setter change, and uncertain preseason roster joins can also imply different prior uncertainty. Rather than immediately changing the prior mean, investigate team-specific uncertainty and a schedule-connectivity diagnostic. This is not approval to activate the held pure-2026 switch or replace its threshold.

**Small trial:** assess uncertainty and calibration by roster-information quality and schedule connectivity, without changing ratings. **Cost/access:** existing graph, priors, and counts. **Success:** identifies systematic overconfidence rather than merely sorting teams differently. **Fallback:** retain the current prior and display a clearly labeled evidence-quality warning.

### 2.6 Fresh diagnostic: four opponent iterations need a convergence receipt

**H — Mathematical implication of the documented update, not a code finding.** If the implementation is exactly a fixed-prior affine update of the form `z_next = a + W P z`, with P a row-normalized opponent matrix and `W_ii = n_i/(n_i+10)`, its contraction bound is `q = max(W_ii) < 1`. Four iterations do not by themselves prove negligible error. For illustration, `0.524^4 ≈ 0.075`, while `0.75^4 ≈ 0.316`; these are bounds on error contraction in a simplified homogeneous case, **not measured POWER errors**.

**R — On a frozen copy, compare four iterations with a tight-residual solution.** Confirm the implementation actually satisfies those assumptions, including missing-opponent handling. Report maximum score differences, rank crossings, and changes in forecast probabilities. Distinguish convergence error from the separate question of whether opponent rescoring improves prediction. **Access/cost:** read-only source and frozen data, local compute. **Small trial:** three season checkpoints; no writes to production. **Success:** a reproducible numerical tolerance certificate. **Fallback:** document measured deviations before deciding whether any change matters.

### 2.7 Passing and the Stanford setter case: measure the missing mechanism

**P — The 510 rows are candidates, not 510 observations of matches.** They contain team and player lines, unresolved aliases, no game-ID joins, and potentially mixed-scope segments. The one-time capture can miss isolated corrections. Poster identity does not establish grader identity. Nothing supports using these rows as a representative league sample. [P0: B024; Passing Quality]

**H — A mean pass grade is an incomplete state description.** On a 0–3 scale, all passes graded 2 and a mixture of one-third 0s plus two-thirds 3s both average 2.0. Those distributions can imply different error exposure and offensive options. A rounded mean and a count generally cannot recover the category counts.

**R — Request counts by grade, explicit grading rules, and setter/attack context in an authorized sample.** Keep the forum grades separate until row-level segment scope, correction chains, identity, and denominator checks pass. Never infer the undefined GP% threshold or convert the five-level paper coding to 0–3 without a documented mapping.

For Stanford, propose **two authorized matches, approximately 200 receptions, and double-coding of a small blinded subset**. Record pass category, setter contact location or a defined quality rubric, attack option, first attack versus transition, hitter, and opponent. Compare the same hitters in similar pass/phase conditions where feasible. A 5-1/6-2 label inferred from starters should be validated against substitutions and actual setter participation.

**Success:** observers can apply the rubric consistently and the evidence distinguishes “poor pass constrained the setter” from “usable pass, poor set” often enough to support an analysis. **Not success:** proving a setter caused wins or losses from two matches. **Fallback:** an annotated case study with explicit uncertainty. Costs are reviewer time and any permissioned-video/data charge, currently unknown; do not buy a team platform solely for this test.

### 2.8 Champions, availability, and the decision standard

**R — Champion profiles need controls.** Compare champion and non-champion teams at matched dates, opponent levels, and sample sizes. Separate selection into the tournament from winning it. The packet's Texas and Texas A&M examples already contradict a universal earned-PPS floor; full-season champion averages are not October prerequisites. Verify the remaining historical sheet before expanding the analysis. [P0: metric 1]

**R — Availability should begin as scenarios, not unexplained deductions.** “With the recent lineup” and “with a documented replacement scenario” are more honest than converting a box-score absence into an injury adjustment. Model uncertainty about participation separately from the uncertain effect of the replacement. Publicly sourced absence does not establish medical cause or causal player value. [P0: metrics 4–6; availability inventory]

**R — Promotion requires more than a positive AUC delta.** Predeclare a target horizon and primary loss, minimum practically useful gain, acceptable calibration, subgroup failure limits, and operating cost. A confidence interval spanning zero means inconclusive—not equivalent. A confidence interval excluding zero is not enough if the gain is tiny, the data rights unclear, or the model fragile. The numerical thresholds remain a joint-review decision.

### 3.3 The vendor/owner questionnaire that prevents an expensive wrong purchase

**R — Require a sample payload, not a feature checklist.** For the same named fixtures, ask for: competition/gender/season coverage; every rally's server; score before and after; substitutions and libero replacements; lineup versus serving-order meaning; reception category/count definitions; set location; attack phase and option; corrections and timestamps; incomplete-match flags; and stable team/player IDs.

## 4. Technology and options matrix

### 4.1 The architecture I would trial—not implement yet

**R — Keep deterministic truth and probabilistic assistance separate.**

`permitted source → immutable observation → deterministic parser/validators → review queue → approved normalized data → model/statistics → published view`

An AI tool may propose field extraction, aliases, claim summaries, or parser repairs. It should not silently approve a correction, assign an injury cause, choose rating weights, or overwrite a historical forecast. All external text is untrusted input; instructions embedded in a page are not project authority. A local LLM “airlock” is an additional heuristic, not a security boundary.

Use a small typed evidence envelope: `source_id`, URL/file reference, event time, observed time, source-updated time if present, content hash, parser/model version, field values, supporting text spans, scope, confidence/abstention, and review state. Keep timestamps unknown when unknown. The reviewer should be able to click a disputed value back to its exact source span.

### 4.3 Existing files + DuckDB: analysis without a rewrite

**V — DuckDB** supports direct JSON and other file ingestion, including analytical use of Parquet. It can be used locally rather than as a hosted database. [S30], [S31]

**R — Use case:** query the existing counts and observations to generate opponent-aware histories, coverage summaries, and matched-date champion comparisons. Build a disposable research database or views from frozen inputs; keep JSONL and existing ledgers authoritative initially. Explicit schemas and canonical IDs matter more than inferred column convenience.

**Access/cost/limits:** local Python-compatible environment and selected project files; no hosted subscription needed for this trial. Benchmark memory and query time on the actual machine. It is not a multiuser transaction service or a provenance system by itself.

**Small trial:** one team-history query and one completeness query, compared exactly with existing Python outputs. **Success:** identical totals and materially simpler investigation. **Fallback:** existing Python. **Maintenance/privacy:** low if read-only and reproducibly rebuilt; no cloud upload. **Priority: TRY NOW.** A transactional queue database is a later option only if concurrent editing or recovery demands it; do not introduce two database systems in the first trial.

## 5. Costs, access, and privacy

### 5.1 A comparable workload—not a promise about the final bill

**R — Example monthly extraction workload:** 1,000 documents × 3,000 input tokens and 300 **billed output tokens** each = **3 million input + 0.3 million output tokens**. This assumes short prompts, standard processing, no caching, no search/tools, no retries, no image/video charges, and no extra reasoning beyond the output-token budget. Tokenizers differ; the same text need not generate the same bill across vendors.

| OpenAI GPT-6 Luna, standard short-context | $0.10 / $0.50 | **$0.45** |
| OpenAI GPT-6 Sol, standard short-context | $2.00 / $10.00 | **$9.00** |

|---|---|

### 5.3 Costs that token arithmetic misses

**Human time:** alias adjudication, grading agreement, parser failures, source disputes, and reviewing false alerts. Track minutes per accepted observation and per useful finding. Cheap extraction that doubles review time is a loss.

**Acquisition and licensing:** Volleymetrics, Balltime's relevant export tier, and Sportradar's target coverage have **unknown applicable prices**. A precise invented monthly fee would be worse than an honest quote-required entry.

**Local compute:** no marginal token invoice does not mean free electricity or unlimited memory. As an illustrative—not measured—electricity scenario, 20W of *additional* continuous load uses 14.4kWh in 30 days, or $4.32 at an assumed $0.30/kWh. The user's actual tariff, duty cycle, and incremental draw are unknown.

**Privacy and rights:** no-training policies, retention, and permission to upload source content are separate questions. Free and paid API tiers may differ. Restrict prompts to necessary public/project material, redact unrelated private data, and never place secrets in generated static assets or public workflow artifacts. A licensed dataset's owner may prohibit cloud processing even if the model vendor offers good privacy controls.

## 6. Proposed small trials and evaluation contracts

These are a **menu for joint selection**, not instructions to run all of them or a newly approved sequence. The first three can yield value using existing data; the contact-data trial tests a different, richer product goal.

### T1 — Freeze and certify the exact baseline

**Question:** can one immutable input snapshot reproduce the documented live rating and its eligibility rules across multiple cutoffs?

**Scope:** three checkpoints; model constants, source hashes, eligible match-ID set, corrections, forecast mapping, and consumer counts. Include same-day verification, start-time corrections, missing boxes, neutral events, and missing-prior opponents. Run the four-iteration convergence diagnostic as a separately labeled check.

**Success:** exact parity within a stated numerical tolerance and no unexplained count divergence. **Failure response:** document the mismatch; do not refit it away. **Cost/access:** local files plus read-only code after approval; $0 service cost. **Owner question:** which frozen checkout is the canonical baseline, given uncommitted start-time/TV work?

### T2 — Repair the evaluation contract before testing improvements

**Question:** which predictions are genuinely comparable and demonstrably pre-match?

**Scope:** select one forecast horizon per match for the primary analysis; preserve other horizons as separate products. Store `prediction_created_at`, intended information cutoff, observed source IDs, model hash, and the fixture/start-time version used. A later corrected start time must not silently rewrite what was known then. Keep input-data and outcome-label versions separately.

**Success:** every scored observation has a defensible eligibility receipt; uncertain timing is excluded or separately reported. Compare log loss, Brier, calibration intercept/slope, and subgroup reliability. Bootstrap matched differences by time blocks, with a 14-day sensitivity analysis and explicit acknowledgment that teams recur across blocks. **Fallback:** prospective-only scored evidence where historical proof is insufficient. Existing verification logs may salvage bounded subsets, not the whole history. **Cost:** local work, no new scheduler required.

### T3 — Existing-data team dossier and 2025 serving study

**Question:** can richer analysis answer useful volleyball questions before it changes POWER?

**Scope:** six teams across schedule strengths and box-coverage conditions; all ten metrics with raw counts, summed-count rates, role/workload context, and missingness. For 20 checked 2025 PBP matches, reconcile score and server transitions, then estimate team points won while each player serves.

**Success:** totals reconcile, opportunity limitations are understandable, and the dossier correctly answers predefined questions about volume versus efficiency. Report stability by opportunities; no universal stabilization threshold. **Fallback:** retain only verified descriptive panels. **Cost:** $0 new subscription; optional DuckDB/OpenVolley evaluation is separate from production dependencies.

### T4 — One predictive candidate, selected after T1/T2 findings

**Choice A:** the scale/population diagnostic in Section 2.1. **Choice B:** one set-aware likelihood. **Choice C:** one lagged, opponent-aware count-feature family. Do not combine all improvements into an uninterpretable candidate.

**Success contract:** freeze the specification, data rules, target horizon, calibration procedure, primary loss, meaningful-gain threshold, and failure limits before forward scoring. Report missing-box, neutral/unknown-venue, early/late, and conference/non-conference behavior. Future untouched data can qualify; “only the rest of 2026 can ever validate it” is too absolute. [P0: Reviewer addendum]

**Fallback:** exact live model. **Cost:** local compute; no license needed for existing counts. **Important:** past exploratory reuse limits confirmatory interpretation even when a bootstrap interval looks favorable.

### T5 — Passing/setter mechanism microstudy

**Question:** does observed passing/set quality explain Stanford's hitter opportunities better than assists or aggregate hitting alone?

**Scope:** two authorized match exports or videos, defined contact rubric, approximately 200 receptions, independent coding on a subset, explicit unknowns and permissions. Do not use the restricted forum as a new acquisition route.

**Success:** match/player attribution, grader agreement, and a usable contrast between reception constraints and setting choices—not a causal verdict or national player ranking. **Fallback:** source-linked qualitative case study. **Cost:** unknown data/video charge; human annotation dominates. **Dependencies:** access and grading definitions, not a new predictive model.

### T6 — Sealed AI extraction benchmark

**Question:** does any AI option reduce work without creating false facts?

**Scope:** 100 previously saved examples spanning source scope, aliases, decimals, absent fields, quoted duplicates, one-line corrections, and prompt injection. Compare deterministic rules, the existing local Qwen model, and **one** cloud API. Include intentionally unanswerable cases. No model sees the answer key.

**Success:** exact-match accuracy on critical IDs/numbers, evidence-span correctness, appropriate abstention, review time, latency, peak memory, and actual cost. A valid JSON response is not a correct response. Require zero unreviewed changes to truth files. **Fallback:** rules/manual queue. **Cost cap proposed:** $10 for the entire cloud trial; none spent now.

### T7 — Operations failure rehearsal

**Question:** will the research pipeline fail visibly rather than silently publish stale work?

**Scope:** controlled offline tests for missed run, duplicate launch, interrupted write, source timeout, empty success response, credential expiry, and stale report. Reuse current execution patterns; do not install another orchestration service.

**Success:** idempotent reruns, no partial publication, distinct source/build/model failure states, and a single actionable review item. A green process exit is insufficient if input freshness failed. **Fallback:** manual run and last-good artifact clearly labeled stale. **Cost:** local time; notification integration deferred until a route is approved.

## 7. Disagreements, unresolved questions, and stop conditions

### 7.1 Conclusions I would challenge in the earlier packet narrative

**“The useful component signal is mostly already inside margin.”** Plausible for some formulations, but not established across role-aware, phase-aware, or availability-aware models. Preserve the narrower tested-result statement.

**“C1 is a simple two-arm floor test.”** Not while population and scale both change. Preserve an exact live arm and recompute the changed-population scale.

**“Venue recovery can only be tested prospectively.”** The existing 2025 files lack venue fields, but recoverable official schedules or archived reports could support a bounded historical subset. No new collection is authorized by this observation.

**“The model has 11,284 held-out matches.”** Not established: forecast checkpoints may repeat target matches. Also, Brier 0.1289 conditioned on true margin is not the same evaluation as a prematch Brier near 0.1718. The reported 2026 scored counts and the inventory's smaller score-file count require snapshot reconciliation. [P0: Reviewer addendum; Current Model §§4, 9; inventory]

**“More source URLs mean independent verification.”** Not necessarily. Two official pages may inherit one underlying scoring error. Preserve source lineage and the correction policy rather than substituting an AI confidence score.

**“A more capable agent solves data acquisition.”** Not without coverage, definitions, permission, and a durable update contract. A consumer app, developer API, MCP server, licensed feed, and local runtime are different products.

### 7.2 Questions for joint review

| Who / area | Decision or evidence still needed |
|---|---|
| Cody: product value | Which first deliverable matters more: a trustworthy team-analysis dossier, the Stanford mechanism case, or a small prospective forecast gain? These need not exclude each other. |
| Builder: baseline | Which immutable checkout and input manifest define live POWER? Can parity be established beyond the single prior cutoff? |
| Reviewer + Builder: evaluation | Which forecast horizon, timing evidence, unique-match denominator, and outcome revision policy will be scored? What practical gain warrants complexity? |
| Builder: operations | What is currently healthy on the volleyball host? Is any Raven infrastructure actually available without personal-data coupling? The September 15 snapshot is not current certification. |
| Data owner/vendor | Can a personal researcher obtain two contact-level files, their definitions, and storage/analysis/publication rights? What exact NCAA women's 2026 coverage exists? |
| Passing reviewers | Can mixed posts be split by segment, isolated corrections recovered, aliases joined, and category denominators reconciled? Who actually graded each report? |
| Model research | Are missing boxes/verification delays related to team strength or conference? Do residual component effects persist forward? Are four iterations numerically sufficient? |
| Product providers | Which actual account quotas and regional terms apply? Why does Firecrawl's current Monitor page show both one and seven credits? |

### 7.3 Specific external facts not confirmed

Pablo's detailed methodology and home coefficient; a reusable license or independently reproduced forecast benchmark for VolleyViz; the Figshare record's DOI/version, exact raw columns, and acquisition method; 2026 in-season ncaavolleyballr publication; a complete licensed NCAA women's contact feed; nationally representative passing-grade coverage; public bulk APIs/export rights for the applicable Hudl plans; and any universal match count at which serving/passing metrics stabilize.

Some endpoints or downloads could not be inspected, including the Figshare raw API/download and attempted current NCAA statistics-manual URLs. Accordingly, exact raw-schema and independently rechecked current-manual claims are not made. The metric definitions used here are explicitly packet-derived or proposed analytical conventions. Search results, vendor marketing, and paper abstracts were not used to fill those gaps silently.

**Stop conditions for any proposed trial:** inaccessible or unlicensed source; ambiguous population; no reliable identity/time contract; silent missing-to-zero conversion; inability to reproduce the baseline; inability to bound spend or tool authority; or operational complexity exceeding the demonstrable benefit.

**Bottom line:** there is credible room for a richer volleyball system. The strongest route is not indiscriminately increasing model complexity. It is to make more of the volleyball mechanism observable, evaluate improvements against an exact temporal baseline, and use narrowly scoped AI to reduce evidence-handling work—not to replace evidence.
